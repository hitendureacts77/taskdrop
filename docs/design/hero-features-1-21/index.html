Hero features: what sells taskdrop.

You asked for a few hero features, with pictures, that would sell the app. Here are eight. Number 1 is the map, which is live. The other seven are ideas: nothing is built, and nothing will be until you pick.

My top three to build first

  1. Money Lock (hero 2), small. The money part already works on staging (Plan B). This gives it a face. It answers the first question on both sides: "what if I pay and he vanishes?" and "what if I work and he does not pay?"

  2. Safe Visit (hero 3), medium. A start code at the door, and a family member can follow the job. It answers the second question: "who is this stranger?" No rival gives the worker a safety contact too.

  3. Say it (hero 4), medium. Speak one sentence in Telugu, Hindi or English and the task is written. The Speak button already exists in English. It is the best 10-second ad of the eight.

Cheap fourth: Photo Receipt (hero 8) is small and rides on the before-and-after photos that are live. Wait for a busy area: Offer Radar, Trust Card and Earn Route look empty when few people are around, so they come after the launch areas fill.

How the store listing would read

One lineSay what you need in your own language, see helpers on a live map, and your money stays locked until the work is done.
Google Play short text (76 of 80 letters)Any task, near you or online. Live map, start code, money locked until done.

What rivals sell (checked on the web, 27 Nov)

  • Urban Company InstaHelp, Snabbit, Pronto: "house help in 10 minutes" from a fixed menu, with their own trained staff, priced by the hour. Snabbit's listing has a start OTP; Pronto sells a recorded "Verified" visit.
  • JustDial, Sulekha, WhatsApp groups: a phone number. No held money, no proof, no help if it goes wrong.
  • Nobody in India sells these together: any task at your own price, a live map for both sides, money locked for odd jobs, your own language, and safety for the worker as well as the customer.

Hero 1 Live todayNothing to build

The live map

See who can help, and what pays, on your own street. Live.

Who it is for

Both. A worker sees the tasks and what each pays. A poster sees the helpers around their home and what each offers.

Why it sells, and the feeling

A menu of services is the same in every city. A map of your own street is personal in one second: "there is ₹450 of work 400 m from me", "14 helpers live around my flat". The feeling is: my area is alive, and help is near. Urban Company, Snabbit and Pronto show a menu. None of them show the people and the prices around you.

How to pitch it as it is today

Make the Earn map the first store picture ("See what pays near you") and the Hire map the second ("14 helpers around your home"). The ad is 10 seconds: open the app, the map flies to your area, the pins pop in. From 27 Nov the Earn pins show the service icon and the price, not the poster's face, so one look says what the job is and what it pays.

Already in the app

Everything: Earn shows tasks only in royal blue, Hire shows helpers only in jade, the pull-up bar, one filter set for the map and the list, areas that open as you zoom, 3D buildings, and it stays smooth with 2,000 pins. Guests can see it before signing up.

Risk

An empty map sells nothing, so the pitch only works in the launch areas where people are close together (decision 38). Spots stay rounded and helpers stay nameless until tapped. Never put fake pins on the real app.

Hero 2 Idea, not builtSize: small

Money Lock

Your money is locked until the work is done. You tap, then they get paid.

Who it is for

Both. The poster sees "my money is safe until I say done". The worker sees "the money is already there before I leave home".

Why it sells, and the feeling

In India the fear is the same on both sides. The customer fears paying an advance and never seeing the person again. The worker fears finishing and then hearing "I will pay tomorrow" or a new, lower price. One picture of a lock with five steps answers both. The feeling is: nobody can cheat me here. Phone-number sites give no protection, and no home-help app shows the worker the money before the trip.

How it attracts

Poster ad: "Pay only when you are happy." Worker ad: "Money locked before you start. No more chasing payments." It also gives a reason to stay on the app instead of swapping numbers.

Already in the app

The Plan B money flow on staging (pay per task through Razorpay), the one-line "where is this task's money" note, the "Your money is protected" line and sheet on Home, refund when a paid task is deleted, and the 3 days to approve. Money Lock is a bigger, clearer face for facts the app already knows.

Risk

These are money promises. The exact words need Razorpay's and the lawyer's written yes, and must say truthfully who holds the money (the payment partner, never a TaskDrop wallet). Never say "guaranteed" or "insured". The fee shown is today's test number and may change after the CA answers.

Hero 3 Idea, not builtSize: medium

Safe Visit

A start code at your door, and your family can follow the job.

Who it is for

Both. The customer letting a stranger into the home, and the worker (often a woman) going into a stranger's home.

Why it sells, and the feeling

After money, safety is the next thing an Indian family asks about. The code proves the right person is at the right door. The shared link means someone at home knows what is happening, without installing anything. The feeling is: I am not doing this alone. Rival apps use a start OTP for their own staff. None give the worker a safety contact, and none do this for any kind of task.

How it attracts

The ad is one line a mother understands: "No code, no entry." For workers: "Someone always knows where you are working."

Already in the app

Swipe to start a job, the four-line safety card before a first in-person job, the task timeline, alerts, the share sheet (WhatsApp and others), and the code boxes used at sign-in. Version 1 shares steps only (hired, on the way, started, done), not a moving dot.

Risk

The Terms today promise no start code and no tracking on purpose (a test checks this), so the legal words change together with the build. The shared link must show the area and the steps only, never the address, and must expire. Call it "an extra check", never "guaranteed safe". TaskDrop is not an emergency service: "Need help" must point to 112 and to support hours that really exist.

Hero 4 Idea, not builtSize: medium

Say it

Just say it, in Telugu, Hindi or English. We write the task.

Who it is for

Posters first: parents, shop owners, anyone who would rather talk than type English. Workers too: a helper from another state reads the task in Hindi, or taps Listen and hears it.

Why it sells, and the feeling

People in India already ask for help with a voice note. Rival apps give an English menu, and if your need is not on the menu you cannot ask. Here you say one sentence the way you would say it to a neighbour, and a clean task appears. The feeling is: this app understands me. The Telugu line in the picture says "Our kitchen tap is leaking. It must be fixed by this evening."

How it attracts

The easiest ad of all eight: a woman says one Telugu sentence into the phone, the task writes itself, a helper's offer arrives. Ten seconds, no reading needed. It fits the tagline: "Ask for anything."

Already in the app

Posting by one sentence with a Speak button (English only today), the writer that turns it into a title, kind, time and budget, and the meaning search that maps "fix my tap" to Plumbing. To add: Telugu and Hindi listening, and the read-in-my-language switch on a task. This is not a translation of the whole app; that is large and can come later.

Risk

The app can hear wrong, so the written task is always shown for a yes before posting. A wrong translation must never change money: prices, times and places are copied, not translated. Each post costs a little AI money, so keep the limits. The rule that blocks phone numbers in tasks must also work in Telugu and Hindi.

Hero 5 Idea, not builtSize: medium

Offer Radar

Post it and watch offers arrive. First offer in minutes.

Who it is for

The poster, in the first ten minutes after posting.

Why it sells, and the feeling

Speed is what the 10-minute apps sell. TaskDrop cannot promise a person at the door in 10 minutes for any task, but it can show speed honestly: who has seen your task, who is writing, and how fast the first price came. Silence after posting is where a new poster gives up. The feeling is: people are already on it.

How it attracts

Ad line: "Posted at 4:02. First offer at 4:04." A screen recording of offers dropping in is the proof. It also makes the map useful again right after posting.

Already in the app

Offers with a price and a time the helper can come, the Compare screen, alerts to helpers nearby, the active-now dot, the map, and the Just posted ticker. To add: a count of real views, and a "writing an offer" signal.

Risk

In a quiet area the radar shows zeros and does harm, so switch it on area by area once enough helpers are active. Every number must be real: no made-up "8 people are looking". Helpers stay nameless until they send an offer. Still no chat before a hire.

Hero 6 Idea, not builtSize: medium

Trust Card

See who your neighbours already hired, before you hire.

Who it is for

The poster choosing between offers. Good workers gain most, because their history finally shows.

Why it sells, and the feeling

In India you hire the person your neighbour used. A 4.8 star rating says little; "12 homes near you hired her, and 17 called her back" says a lot. This turns word of mouth into a number, with no group, no chat and no names. The feeling is: people like me already trust this person.

How it attracts

Ad line: "Hired 12 times on your street." It is also a reason for workers to do every job through the app: each one adds to the card.

Already in the app

Levels (Starter, Trusted, Pro, Top Dropper), reviews with praise words (On time, Careful work, Fair price), Regulars (hire the same helper again), phone and email checks, before-and-after photos with the customer's permission, and rounded locations to count from.

Risk

Privacy: show counts only, only from 3 upward, never who or which building. "ID checked" may appear only when a real check ran; today the only check is the PAN a worker gives Razorpay to get paid, and an Aadhaar or DigiLocker check costs money per person and needs the lawyer. Never write "police verified". New helpers start at zero, so show a kind "New here" instead of empty tiles.

Hero 7 Idea, not builtSize: medium to large

Earn Route

See today's earning on one route: 3 jobs, 4 km, ₹1,350.

Who it is for

The worker planning a day: students, people between jobs, skilled workers filling empty hours.

Why it sells, and the feeling

A worker does not ask "is there a job?". They ask "how much can I make today without wasting petrol?". The map answers with one number and one route. Delivery apps decide the route for the rider; here the worker chooses. The feeling is: I am in charge of my day, and I can see the money. It makes the map a daily habit, not a one-time look.

How it attracts

Worker ad: "₹1,350 of work within 4 km of you today." It is the map hero, pointed at earning.

Already in the app

The Earn map with price pins, one filter set for map and list, the custom distance, finish times on tasks, offers, and the weekly goal on the Streaks screen. Version 1 can order jobs by time and straight-line distance with no paid routing service.

Risk

It is a plan, not income: an offer can be refused, so it always says "if you are hired". Task spots are rounded until hired, so distance is rough. Real road routes cost money per request; leave them out of version 1. Do not nudge a worker to take more jobs than fit in the day, or late jobs will hurt reviews.

Hero 8 Idea, not builtSize: small

Photo Receipt

Every job ends with a photo receipt: before, after, paid.

Who it is for

Both. The poster gets proof to keep or forward (to a landlord, an office, a parent in another city). The worker gets a wall of real work that wins the next job.

Why it sells, and the feeling

Proof you can see beats any promise. A before-and-after picture with the price and the date is a small moment of pride for both people. The feeling is: it is really done, and I can show it. Rival apps send an invoice email. Nobody sends something you want to forward.

How it attracts

Every receipt shared on WhatsApp is a free ad with real work in it and "Done on taskdrop." at the bottom. That is how the app spreads inside a building without any group feature.

Already in the app

Before-and-after photos on a finished job with the customer's permission and the drag-to-compare view, reviews, the share sheet, photo storage, and Regulars for "book again". To add: one tidy card and its share picture.

Risk

Photos of a home are private: only the poster can share the receipt, and the worker's wall needs the poster's yes (already the rule). No address and no faces on the card. Call it a "job receipt", not a tax invoice, until the CA says what an invoice needs. Photo storage has a free limit to watch.

Rules every idea here keeps

  • Nothing is built before you confirm the design.
  • No chat before a hire. Questions on a task stay as they are.
  • No communities or groups. "Hired near you" is a count, not a place to talk.
  • Hire is always jade, Earn is always royal blue. Gold means verified only.
  • Map spots stay rounded, helpers stay nameless until tapped.
  • Tasks stay local and remote. The map is the local half; remote work keeps its list.
  • Every number shown to a user is real. No fake pins, views or counts.

Already live, and worth a line in the pitch too

  • City Hunt: put a reward on finding something; pay only the person who finds it.
  • Big Days: plan a house move or a wedding at home as one board of tasks.
  • Regulars: found a good helper? Book them again; they get the first 10 minutes.
  • Guest mode: look at the map and the tasks before making an account.
